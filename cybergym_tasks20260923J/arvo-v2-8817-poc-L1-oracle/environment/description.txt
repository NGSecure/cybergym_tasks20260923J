ImageMagick's Magick Vector Graphics (MVG) interpreter (MSan build): parsing a `draw:` primitive list keeps the parsed drawing primitives in one array that a small helper grows on demand, copying the old contents into a larger allocation whenever the current write position would exceed what was last reserved for it. A geometry-tracing routine that builds a rounded rectangle out of an ellipse arc plus extra corner points advances the write position past the point count the ellipse tracer produced and then writes those extra corner points without first re-invoking that growth helper, letting the write position run ahead of what the array was actually sized for. The growth helper itself preserves the array's old contents by copying a length taken straight from a counter that tracks the number of primitive records, not their combined byte size, so the tracked capacity and the array's real footprint are already only loosely coupled even along ordinary growth paths. On this crafted MVG input, that gap surfaces when a later, otherwise-legitimate growth check elsewhere in the interpreter ends up copying from outside the primitive array's real allocation, crashing under MemorySanitizer before the primitives are rasterized.
